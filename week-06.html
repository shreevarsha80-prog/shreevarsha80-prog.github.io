UNDERSTANDING LASER CUTTING

What is Laser Cutting?

Laser cutting is a digital fabrication process that uses a focused laser beam to cut, engrave, or mark materials according to a digital design. The laser is computer-controlled, allowing accurate and repeatable reproduction of digital designs with high precision.

How it Works

1 Digital Design Digital artwork reference
2 File Prep Vector cleaning & scaling
3 DXF Vector Coordinate geometry
4 RDWorks Layer CAM setup
5 Settings Speed & power limits
6 Laser Process CO₂ beam execution
7 Physical Part Finished keychain

Cutting vs. Scanning / Engraving

Cutting

The laser passes through the material to separate a section.

Scanning / Engraving

The laser marks or removes material from the surface without completely cutting through it.

Types of Laser Systems

CO₂ Laser

Infrared gas laser best suited for organic and non-metallic media like acrylic, timber, and paper.

Fiber Laser

Solid-state fiber system tailored for sheet metal fabrication and industrial marking.

Diode Laser

Compact semiconductor laser utilized for desktop engraving and lightweight hobby materials.

The machine used for my activity was a 1490 CO₂ Laser.

Before Fabrication

Safety Goggles: Wear certified eye protection.
Gloves: Wear gloves during sheet loading and unloading.
Correct Settings: Use proper settings to avoid fire hazards.
Blower/Exhaust: Keep the lower blowing exhaust running.
Chiller: Water chiller must be active to cool the tube.
Door Closed: Keep machine door closed during operation.
Attentive: Never leave the laser cutter unattended.
Power Off: Do not switch off directly while operating.
Authorized Only: Avoid materials that release toxic fumes.
Ask Staff: Consult technical staff when unsure.
Clean Workspace: Clean the work area after use and clear offcuts.

UNDERSTANDING 3D PRINTING

3D printing is an additive manufacturing process. Objects are created layer by layer from a digital 3D model. Unlike subtractive manufacturing (such as CNC milling or carving), where material is removed from a solid block, additive manufacturing builds geometry by depositing material precisely only where needed.

General Workflow Pipeline
3D MODEL → SLICING → PRINTER SETUP → LAYER-BY-LAYER PRINTING → FINAL OBJECT
STL (Stereolithography)

A common file format used to represent the surface geometry of a 3D object as a triangular mesh.

DESIGNING FOR STRENGTH

Infill

Infill is the internal structure inside a 3D printed object and affects weight, strength, and print time. Rather than printing solid plastic, a geometric pattern is generated inside the walls.

Triangle Infill

Provides balanced multi-directional stability and high stiffness with moderate print time.

Honeycomb Infill

My observation was that while triangle infill provides stability, honeycomb can provide greater structural stability depending on the application.

Directional Pressures & Orientation

Vertical Pressure

Force acting along the height/orientation of the object.

Horizontal Pressure

Force acting across the object.

Because 3D printed objects are built layer by layer, print orientation and internal structure strongly determine where the part can resist force and prevent delamination under mechanical load.

WHAT I LEARNED

Working with the Bambu Lab H2S taught me the foundational principles of additive manufacturing. I learned how a digital 3D model becomes printable through slicing, converting mesh polygons into toolpaths that the machine executes layer by layer.

I understood the importance of infill geometry and structural design—how triangle versus honeycomb patterns balance weight, printing time, and rigidity, and how horizontal and vertical forces require thoughtful print orientation to prevent delamination.

Preparing the model in Bambu Studio also highlighted how weight constraints directly shape design decisions: resizing the downloaded model allowed me to keep the holder durable while bringing the final piece to approximately 27 g—well under the 50 g target. Most importantly, I learned the critical distinction between simply downloading a 3D model and systematically preparing it for real-world fabrication.
