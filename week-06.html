My First Experience with Laser Cutting

SV
Shree Varsha V K
ProtoSem 25.2 • Forge Innovation & Ventures

This week, I got to try something completely new — laser cutting.

Sabresh introduced us to the basics of laser cutting, the different types, how the machine works, and the safety precautions we need to follow. We also learned how to use RDWorks, the software used to prepare designs for the laser cutter.

Operating the laser cutting machine

For the practical activity, we had to choose an image and turn it into a physical design. I chose Van Gogh’s The Starry Night because I wanted to see how a painting would translate into a laser-cut design.

Van Gogh’s The Starry Night

We first converted the JPG image into a DXF file using Converto and then imported it into RDWorks. From there, we worked on the design, assigned different colours for cutting and scanning, and added text to it.

Design prepared in RDWorks with cutting and scanning layers

Learning Through My First Attempt

My first attempt did not go as planned. I forgot to mirror the design before printing, and the result was completely wrong. the letters came out reversed and the design looked nothing like what I had expected.

Thankfully, I got a second chance to print it. This time, I corrected the orientation and mirrored the design before sending it to the machine. The second output came out beautifully and much closer to what I had imagined.

First Attempt
First attempt — letters came out reversed (forgot to mirror)
Second Attempt
Second attempt — orientation corrected and mirrored

The Final Product

The final design was made on a black plastic board and turned into a keychain. The actual laser-processing took less than five minutes.

The final keychain made on black plastic board

What I enjoyed most was seeing the entire journey from an image on a screen → a prepared digital file → a machine → a physical product. The failed first attempt also taught me that even a small step in the preparation process can completely change the final result.
