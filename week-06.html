UNDERSTANDING LASER CUTTING

What is Laser Cutting?

Laser cutting is a digital fabrication process that uses a focused laser beam to cut, engrave, or mark materials according to a digital design. The laser is computer-controlled, allowing accurate and repeatable reproduction of digital designs with high precision.

How it Works

1 Digital Design Digital artwork reference
2 File Prep Vector cleaning & scaling
3 DXF Vector Coordinate geometry
4 RDWorks Layer CAM setup
5 Settings Speed & power limits
6 Laser Process CO₂ beam execution
7 Physical Part Finished keychain

Cutting vs. Scanning / Engraving

Cutting

The laser passes through the material to separate a section.

Scanning / Engraving

The laser marks or removes material from the surface without completely cutting through it.

Types of Laser Systems

CO₂ Laser

Infrared gas laser best suited for organic and non-metallic media like acrylic, timber, and paper.

Fiber Laser

Solid-state fiber system tailored for sheet metal fabrication and industrial marking.

Diode Laser

Compact semiconductor laser utilized for desktop engraving and lightweight hobby materials.

The machine used for my activity was a 1490 CO₂ Laser.

Before Fabrication

Safety Goggles: Wear certified eye protection.
Gloves: Wear gloves during sheet loading and unloading.
Correct Settings: Use proper settings to avoid fire hazards.
Blower/Exhaust: Keep the lower blowing exhaust running.
Chiller: Water chiller must be active to cool the tube.
Door Closed: Keep machine door closed during operation.
Attentive: Never leave the laser cutter unattended.
Power Off: Do not switch off directly while operating.
Authorized Only: Avoid materials that release toxic fumes.
Ask Staff: Consult technical staff when unsure.
Clean Workspace: Clean the work area after use and clear offcuts.

THE STARRY NIGHT → JPG → CONVERTIO → DXF → RDWORKS
Actual Process:
  1. Selected The Starry Night image.
  2. Used Converto to convert the JPG into DXF.

Why the Layers Matter

Different colours were used to distinguish different laser operations—assigning red for vector cutting through the 2 mm acrylic boundary and black for surface raster scanning/engraving the artwork and text.

Final design layout and operation layers prepared in RDWorks.

FABRICATION

THE FIRST ATTEMPT

The First Attempt:

I forgot to mirror the design before laser processing. As a result, the letters appeared reversed and the output was not as intended.

The Fix:

I corrected the orientation and mirrored the design in RDWorks before running the process again.

The Second Attempt:

The second output came out correctly oriented and much better than the first.

WHAT I LEARNED

This activity was my first hands-on experience with laser cutting, and I found it interesting to see how a digital image could be turned into a physical product.

I also learned that laser cutting is not simply about placing material inside a machine. The design preparation, file format, orientation, material, settings and safety procedures all affect the final result.
