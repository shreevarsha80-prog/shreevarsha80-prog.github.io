01 — Introduction to Laser Cutting

Learning

This week, I got to try something completely new — laser cutting.

Sabresh introduced us to the basics of laser cutting — the different types, how the machine works, and the safety precautions we need to follow. We also learned how to use RDWorks, the software used to prepare designs for the laser cutter.

Operating the laser cutter at the Forge fabrication lab

Choosing My Design

For the practical activity, we had to choose an image and turn it into a physical design. I chose Van Gogh's The Starry Night because I wanted to see how a painting would translate into a laser-cut design.

Van Gogh's "The Starry Night" — my chosen reference image
Key Concept:

Laser cutting uses a focused beam of light to cut or engrave materials with precision. Different colours in the design file represent different operations — cutting vs. scanning (engraving).

02 — The Digital Workflow

Process

We first converted the JPG image into a DXF file using Converto and then imported it into RDWorks. From there, we worked on the design, assigned different colours for cutting and scanning, and added text to it.

DXF design in RDWorks — vector outlines with "Van gogh starry night" text and keychain hole
Workflow Note:

Converting a raster image (JPG) to vector (DXF) is a critical step — it translates pixels into mathematical paths the laser can follow precisely.

03 — Learning Through My First Attempt

Iteration

My first attempt did not go as planned. I forgot to mirror the design before printing, and the result was completely wrong — the letters came out reversed and the design looked nothing like what I had expected.

Thankfully, I got a second chance to print it. This time, I corrected the orientation and mirrored the design before sending it to the machine. The second output came out beautifully and much closer to what I had imagined.

Attempt 01 — Mirrored
Text came out reversed — forgot to mirror
Attempt 02 — Corrected
Corrected orientation — "Van gogh starry night" reads perfectly
Lesson Learned:

Even a small step in the preparation process — like forgetting to mirror — can completely change the final result. The failed first attempt taught me to always double-check orientation before sending a design to the machine.

05 — What I Enjoyed Most

Reflection

What I enjoyed most was seeing the entire journey — from an image on a screen, to a prepared digital file, to a machine, to a physical product.

The failed first attempt also taught me that even a small step in the preparation process can completely change the final result. It's a lesson that applies far beyond laser cutting — in any process where preparation matters, which is essentially everything.

Image on Screen → Digital File (DXF) → RDWorks Design → Laser Machine → Physical Keychain ✨
Week 6 Takeaway:

Making things tangible is deeply satisfying. And mistakes in the process aren't setbacks — they're the best teachers. Every mirrored letter, every reversed design is a lesson that sticks.
