WEEK 0 — THE JOURNEY BEGINS ✈️

“The first landing of my PRICE ProtoSem journey—a week that made me look inward, look around, and finally start looking at what I could create.”

SV
Shree Varsha V K
Innovation Engineer Trainee • PRICE ProtoSem

Day 1 — Finding My Place

Day 01/05

Morning — Starting With Introductions

The first day of PRICE Protosem began with something simple—introducing ourselves.

We were made to sit in a circle, where each of us introduced ourselves so that the person next to us could get to know who we were. When it was my turn, I felt quite confident speaking about myself. It was a small beginning, but it felt like the first step into a completely new journey.

Afternoon — From Ideas to Reality

The afternoon took us from conversations to making. We explored different labs and got a glimpse of the tools that turn ideas into something tangible.

We saw 3D printing machines, laser-cutting processes, and different prototyping facilities. Watching an idea take shape physically made the concept of prototyping feel much more real to me.

Day 1 Takeaway:

The journey began with knowing the people around me—and then seeing how ideas can actually take shape.

Day 2 — Wonder, Identity & Discovering Myself

Day 02/05

Morning — Finding a Story That Felt Like Me

We were asked to pick a comic story from Zenzi Pencils, and I chose Wonder & Excitement.

The story spoke about preserving the sense of wonder that we have as children—the curiosity that makes us see something new and immediately think, “I want to try that.”

That idea felt very close to me because wonder and excitement are two things that naturally drive me. Whenever I come across something new or different, I feel the urge to explore it. If something excites me, I want to go and try it. And if something else looks completely different, I want to explore that too.

Maybe that curiosity is one of the things that keeps me moving.

Another Story — Mary Shelley’s Teenage Dream

I also shared Mary Shelley’s Teenage Dream. What connected with me was the pressure of expectations—knowing that people around you are constantly asking where you are heading, while you are still trying to figure things out yourself.

The struggles of the author, the pressure of having to create something, and the feeling of being unable to produce what is expected of you made me reflect on my own experiences. It made me think about how often we are asked: “What do you want to become?” even when we are still discovering what genuinely excites us.

Discovering My Personality

We also took the 16Personalities test, and I received the Campaigner (ENFP) personality.

It gave me another perspective on myself—my curiosity, enthusiasm, openness to new experiences, and the way I naturally get excited about possibilities. At the same time, I realised that the way we behave can also change depending on the environment, the people around us, and the experiences we go through. Our personality is not just a label; it is also something we understand better through the situations we experience.

Understanding LinkedIn

The day also introduced me to LinkedIn and how it works as a platform for building and communicating our professional identity. I learned more about how people present their experiences, skills, achievements, and interests—and how all these pieces eventually become part of our professional story.

Day 2 Takeaway:

I discovered that my curiosity isn't just something I have—it might actually be one of the things that drives me.

Day 3 — Choices, Strategy & Teamwork

Day 03/05

Morning — The Marshmallow Challenge

Day 3 started with the Marshmallow Challenge. I arrived a little late because I had a meeting with my mentor. But even being late became a small lesson for me—every day is filled with choices, and sometimes the choices we make also mean missing out on something else.

Even though I joined late, our team quickly got into the challenge. We built a 52 cm tower, which placed us first in the challenge. It was a simple activity, but it showed me how quickly a team can adapt, divide responsibilities, and work towards a common goal.

One funny moment I remember was Tarunika taking the marshmallow, removing its centre, and sharing pieces with everyone. I couldn't eat any because I had a cough, but it definitely added to the fun of the activity.

Among Us — Observe Before You Assume

We also played an Among Us-inspired game, and this turned out to be much more about observation and strategy than I expected.

In the first round, we weren't the imposters. We started watching the reactions of people as they opened their chits. Whenever someone called the mentor to ask what was written on their chit, we realised that they probably weren't the imposter, because the imposter already knew their role. By combining these observations, we identified the correct imposter team.

In the second round, our Beta Team became the imposters! I came up with a plan to collect everyone's water bottles and place them in the centre to make it look like a team task. It wasn't really about winning—it was about creating an experience together.

Beta Team — The Beginning of a Team

Day 3 marked the formation of our Beta Team. I joined Tarunika, Visakan, Maha, and Affrina, and this was the beginning of working together as a team rather than simply participating as individuals.

Day 3 Takeaway:

From building a 52 cm tower to decoding people's reactions, Day 3 reminded me that good teamwork is a mix of observation, strategy, adaptability, and a little bit of fun.

Day 4 — Taking the Stage & Thinking Beyond the Obvious

Day 04/05

Preparing for the Inauguration

Day 4 was largely focused on preparing for the PRICE Protosem inauguration in the evening. As a member of the Voice & Views team, I had the opportunity to step onto the stage and speak about what I expected from the programme.

For me, two things stood out—interdisciplinary learning and networking. I was excited about learning with people from different disciplines, because every field brings a different way of looking at the same problem.

Evening — From Consumer Problems to Wild Ideas

We also had an engaging session with CEO Rajagopalan, where we were encouraged to look at problems from the consumer's perspective.

Instead of immediately asking what we could build, we were encouraged to first understand the problems and pain points consumers actually face and then think about how we could create meaningful solutions around them.

What stood out to me was the encouragement to think about wild ideas—ideas that might initially seem unusual or even unrealistic. He also shared his experience connecting physical and digital retail, helping me understand the essence of phygital retail.

Day 4 Takeaway:

Don't begin by asking, “What can we build?” Begin by asking, “What problem are people actually experiencing?”

Day 5 — Learning From Those Who Took the Path

Day 05/05

Accelerator Program — Kickoff 2026

Day 5 began with the Accelerator Program Kickoff 2026, where we had the opportunity to listen to R. Krishnan, Managing Director of Tulasi Pharmacy.

What stayed with me was his journey—from starting out as a medical representative to eventually taking on a leadership role. It reminded me that successful careers rarely follow a straight line; they are built through setbacks, persistence, and seizing opportunities.

An Unexpected Connection — An Alumna on Stage

We also had Swati Shree, founder of The Toilet Thing, join us. I had already seen her on Shark Tank India, but what made the moment even more memorable was discovering that she is an alumna of our own college. Seeing someone who had once been a student in the same institution standing on that stage as a founder made entrepreneurship feel much closer to home.

Closing Week 0 — Looking Back

The day ended with our presentation of everything we had worked on throughout the week. The week slowly moved from:

“Who am I?” → “What excites me?” → “How do I work with others?” → “What problems can I solve?” → “What can I create?”

And perhaps that is what made Week 0 special. It didn't give me all the answers. It made me more curious about the questions.

Day 5 Takeaway:

The journey doesn't become inspiring because the destination is impressive. It becomes inspiring because of everything you learn, overcome, and discover along the way.
